# Vision development

22–23 October 2026 · How the vision statement moved, and why

## Where it started

> If our relationship teams knew what the firm knows, they would be three times as productive.

Written for the pivot (`pivot.md`). Review found two problems: "three times" was an unsupported claim, and "what the firm knows" was vague about where the knowledge comes from.

## Step 1: soften the claim

> If our relationship teams knew what the firm knows, they would be far more productive. The pilot's target is three times; the pilot measures it.

The 3× became a pilot target rather than a promise. The sentence still did not say what the firm should know, or why it matters beyond productivity.

## Step 2: agentic teams, and a placeholder

> If our agentic teams knew what … knows, they would be far more productive.

Two changes. **Relationship teams** became **agentic teams**, because the accelerator puts AI agents into the work, not beside it. The **…** asked for something specific: the idea is to understand, and maximize, the value the firm gets from customer interactions.

## Step 3: candidates for the placeholder

| Candidate | Verdict |
|---|---|
| every advisor interaction has taught the firm | Accurate, but too verbose |
| our advisors have told us | Liked for its brevity; misses the point |
| the firm has heard across every advisor conversation | Too long |
| each client conversation reveals | Short, but vague |

An alternative ending was also offered and kept: **"no conversation would go to waste"** replaces "they would be far more productive", because it speaks to value, not just effort.

## Step 4: the real goal

"Our advisors have told us" was rejected because it stops at listening. The goal is larger:

- **Know the advisor in time**: what they said, use, want and are worried about, while it still matters.
- **Recommend just in time**: the advisor's next move, including career moves, before anyone else does.
- **Retain the best talent on the firm's platform.**
- **Grow together**: when talented advisors increase their revenue, the firm's revenue increases.

## Step 5: the working vision

> **If our agentic teams knew what each advisor needs next, no conversation would go to waste.**
>
> *Know every advisor in time, recommend their next move before anyone else does, and keep our best talent growing on our platform: when they grow, the firm grows.*

"What each advisor needs next" carries timing (know them in time) and action (the recommendation), and stays short. The business outcome moves into one supporting line, so the headline does not have to carry it.

Headline alternatives kept on the table:

- "…knew **where each advisor is heading**…": more weight on career trajectory and retention.
- "…knew **our advisors as they are today**…": more weight on in-time knowledge, less on what comes next.

## Definition: agentic teams

> *Agentic teams: subject-matter-expert teams with AI agents embedded across the whole advisor lifecycle, and a human in the middle of every decision.*

"Human in the middle", not the usual "human in the loop": people decide, agents prepare. It matches the product's one gate, where an SME approves every finding before it enters the firm's memory.

## Scope decision: next best action on the roadmap

The new vision promises just-in-time recommendations, including career moves, and talent retention. The pivot had put career-pattern analytics and predictive scoring out of scope. Two options were weighed:

- **(a) Keep the scope; put next best action on the roadmap.** Chosen.
- (b) Pull a first career signal, such as affiliation changes or exam milestones, into scope now.

So the first release delivers the foundation: knowing each advisor in time, from expert-approved conversations, with every promise owned. **Next best action**, including career recommendations, builds on that foundation in a later phase, and the documents should say so.
